### Reading the yearly profiles

Each line in this chart represents a **complete calendar year**, plotted from January through December using monthly mean temperatures. Reading a single line from left to right shows how temperatures typically rise and fall across the seasons for that year.

When many years are shown together, the individual lines begin to form a **band**. This band represents the range of monthly mean temperatures observed across the record at each point in the year. Where the band is narrow, different years tend to behave similarly for that month; where it widens, there is greater variation between years.

This view is particularly useful for comparing **seasonal shape** — for example, whether warming or cooling is concentrated in certain months, or whether recent years sit consistently higher or lower within the historical range. However, as more years are added, it becomes harder to see how all years relate to one another as a whole.

The next view builds on this same data by arranging the yearly profiles into a surface. This makes it easier to see how patterns evolve across time and to identify regions where smoothing across years may help highlight longer-term structure.
